Garbage Disposal Repair in Austin, TX

Disposal repairs for jams, humming motors, leaks, and backed-up kitchen sinks.

Call (512) 592-6789 Ask a question

Older kitchens in Austin, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills, and Lost Creek often have a disposal that has outlasted the original sink flange, drain fittings, or switch wiring. The unit may have worked through years of food scraps and dishwasher discharge before a worn motor, loose mounting ring, or blocked grind chamber finally shows up at the kitchen sink. We check the disposal, the drain connection, and the electrical feed under the cabinet rather than treating every stopped unit as the same problem.

A disposal can stop after a spoon, fruit pit, bottle cap, or dense food waste jams the grinding plate. It can also hum with no movement because the motor cannot turn, trip its reset button because of heat, or leak at the dishwasher inlet, discharge elbow, sink flange, or body seam. Water in the cabinet is not always coming from the disposal itself; the P-trap, slip-joint washer, or drain line beside it can make the source hard to identify without running the sink.

This is not work to put off while the kitchen sink is still being used. A jammed or leaking disposal can hold water in the sink, send water onto the cabinet base, or allow moisture to reach the motor and wiring beneath the bowl. We recommend stopping use of the unit and avoiding the sink if water is backing up or appearing under the cabinet.

Humming disposal and sink odor

People often call us because the disposal hums but does not spin, makes a grinding or rattling sound, or simply goes silent when the wall switch is turned on. A humming sound can point to a jammed grinding plate or a motor that cannot start. Silence can come from a tripped reset, a switch issue, a loose connection, or a failed motor. A repeated clicking sound may come from an object moving around the grind chamber.

Other common reports are a kitchen sink that drains slowly after the disposal runs, dirty water rising into the other sink bowl, a sour odor from the drain opening, or water dripping into the cabinet. Food caught around the splash guard can cause an odor, while a blocked discharge tube or downstream drain can leave water in the basin. These symptoms can look alike from above the sink, but the failed part, labor involved, and replacement material can be very different once we inspect the unit.

Disposal inspection at the sink

At the sink, we first isolate the power and check the wall switch, reset button, cord or hardwired connection, and motor response. We inspect the splash guard and grind chamber for foreign objects, then check the mounting ring, sink flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge elbow, P-trap, and drain line for moisture or movement. Where accessible, we run water through both sink bowls and watch the drain path instead of assuming a cabinet drip comes from one fitting.

Initial electrical and drain checks commonly take 20 to 40 minutes, depending on cabinet access and whether water is standing in the sink. A loose connection, tripped reset, accessible jam, worn gasket, or drain fitting can often be addressed during the same visit when the needed part is on hand. If the motor has failed, the mounting assembly is corroded, or the replacement must match a particular sink opening, dishwasher connection, or existing drain layout, we identify what is needed and arrange the follow-up work after the correct unit or part is available.

Motor age and cabinet access

The condition and age of the motor are major factors on this job. A disposal that is roughly 8 to 15 years old may have a worn motor, damaged internal components, or corrosion around the housing, which can make replacement more practical than repeated work on the existing unit. A recent unit with a jammed chamber, loose wire, or failed reset may call for a much smaller repair scope.

Cabinet access also changes the work involved. A clear cabinet with visible shutoff valves, a reachable outlet, and a standard 1 1/2-inch tubular drain connection is simpler to inspect than a cabinet packed with shelving, water-damaged flooring, rigid pipe, or a seized connection. A failed mounting ring, corroded sink flange, stuck shutoff, or model-specific part that must be ordered adds parts and return work beyond a basic jam check.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, check the model number and make on the disposal label, which is often printed on the side or bottom of the housing under the sink. Let us know which kitchen sink bowl holds water and whether the disposal hums, clicks, leaks, or stays completely silent. If you know when the unit was installed, that age can help distinguish an isolated jam from an aging motor. Clear a path to the sink cabinet if possible, and tell us whether the cabinet floor is wet or water is running now.
