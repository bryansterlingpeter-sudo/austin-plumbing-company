Drain Cleaning in Austin, TX

We clear slow, backed-up, and foul-smelling drains in Austin and nearby areas.

Call (512) 592-6789 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles, a shower that holds dirty water, or a sewer odor near a floor drain usually points to a drain line that is no longer moving water freely. Grease, soap residue, hair, food scraps, and other buildup can narrow the pipe until water slows down or returns through the lowest fixture. We cover drain problems in Austin, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills, and Lost Creek, starting with the fixture and following the route toward the accessible cleanout.

A slow drain can sometimes wait briefly if water still leaves the fixture and no other drain is affected, but a backup, repeated gurgling, or sewer smell should not be left alone. Water standing in a sink, tub, or floor drain can bring debris and wastewater back into the room, while a blockage farther down the line can spread to another fixture when more water enters the pipe. The sooner the obstruction is located, the less likely the backup is to move beyond one drain opening.

Gurgling sinks and sewer odor

Customers often call because the kitchen sink takes several minutes to empty, the bathtub fills around their feet, or the shower drain bubbles after the toilet is flushed. A drain may also make a hollow glugging sound, send water into a nearby fixture, or leave a sewage-like odor around the laundry standpipe or floor drain. In a kitchen line, greasy water and food debris may be the issue; in a bathroom branch, hair and soap buildup are common; outdoors, roots or a damaged pipe can affect the main route.

Those symptoms can look alike from the room, but the obstruction may sit in the trap, the branch line, the vent connection, or farther along the sewer pipe. A reachable clog at one fixture involves different equipment and access than a blockage beyond a cleanout or under a slab. We inspect the route before recommending work because the source of the symptom, not just the water level in the bowl or sink, determines what the job involves.

Drain line inspection

At the visit, we begin with the affected fixture, run water where appropriate, and check whether nearby sinks, tubs, or floor drains react. We inspect the trap, drain opening, overflow, and accessible cleanout for buildup, loose fittings, or signs that water has been standing in the pipe. A basic fixture check commonly takes around 20 to 40 minutes before clearing begins, depending on access and whether water is present in more than one room. We also note the pipe material and diameter, since a narrow branch line and a larger sewer line require different cable heads and cleaning methods.

When the blockage is reachable, we can commonly clear the line through the drain opening, trap arm, or cleanout during the same visit, then run water to see whether the fixture drains normally. A cable machine can break through hair, grease, soap residue, and compacted debris, while a camera inspection can show whether roots, a separated joint, or a damaged section is farther down the route. If the pipe needs replacement, excavation, or access through a wall, floor, or slab, that work is planned separately after the line and route have been assessed.

Pipe access and blockage depth

The location of the blockage changes the work involved. A clog close to a sink trap or a clear exterior cleanout is usually more direct to reach than one deep in a branch line, beneath a slab, or beyond a shared connection. Access also matters: a cleanout behind stored boxes, a drain opening under a heavy appliance, or a pipe concealed in a wall can add preparation before the line can be worked on.

Pipe material and condition can also affect the approach. Older cast iron may hold scale inside the pipe, while roots can enter through joints in a buried sewer line and require inspection after the water begins moving again. If a camera shows a broken pipe, offset joint, or collapsed section rather than removable buildup, clearing the opening may only be the first step and repair work may need to be arranged separately.

Fixture location and cleanout access

Before calling, note which room and fixture is affected, such as the kitchen sink, upstairs shower, laundry standpipe, or garage floor drain. Let us know whether one drain is slow or whether water rises in another fixture when you run the faucet, flush, or use the washing machine. If you know where the exterior or interior cleanout is located, describe whether it is clear of furniture, landscaping, storage, or parked vehicles. It also helps to mention whether water is currently standing in the basin, tub, or floor drain and whether you have used a chemical drain product.
